# Inovix product briefs: seed material for the market simulation
(Inspired by the MiroFish startup-simulation guide. Edit these as you learn. Prices marked
"assumed" are guesses for the simulation, not real quotes. Real evidence goes in the Evidence
panel; the simulation reads it too. No personal data or secrets here.)

## Wattly
- Idea: a small device on the home meter (ESP32 + current sensor) with an app that predicts the monthly bill and warns before the household crosses the next tariff slab.
- Target customer: middle-class households and small shops in Pakistani cities who check their electricity bill every month and fear crossing the 200 or 300 unit slab.
- Problem and current behavior: bills jump when usage crosses a slab. Today people read the meter by hand, guess, or find out when the bill arrives. Some use free utility apps that only show past bills.
- Evidence: none recorded yet (prototype tested by the team).
- Offer (assumed): device PKR 6,500 one-time, installed by an electrician, plus an optional app plan of PKR 300 a month for AI tips and alerts. Sold online and through electronics shops.
- Assumptions to test: 1) people will pay for a device, not only a free app; 2) the slab alert changes behavior enough to save more than it costs; 3) installation on the meter is acceptable and safe; 4) households trust the bill prediction.

## Fabriq AI
- Idea: a camera and AI model on the inspection table or loom that spots fabric defects (holes, stains, weft bars, broken ends) in real time.
- Target customer: owners and production managers of small and medium weaving and textile mills in Faisalabad, inspecting fabric every shift.
- Problem and current behavior: inspection is manual, workers miss defects when tired, and rejected export lots cost a lot. Big mills buy expensive imported systems; small mills can't afford them.
- Evidence: none recorded yet (prototype detects common defects on sample fabric).
- Offer (assumed): pilot on one inspection table for PKR 150,000 setup, then PKR 25,000 a month per table, one-day installation, no need to stop the looms for long.
- Assumptions to test: 1) mill owners believe the AI catches more than their checkers; 2) they will pay before seeing savings; 3) install fits a one-day window; 4) dust, lighting and fabric types don't break accuracy.

## Kidz Lab
- Idea: an AI tutor that teaches kids aged 9 to 14 Python and basic AI with projects, in English and Urdu.
- Target customer: private schools that want a coding club, and parents in cities who pay for extra classes.
- Problem and current behavior: schools lack trained computer teachers; parents pay for tuition centers or YouTube. Few courses fit Pakistani schools and budgets.
- Evidence: none recorded yet (no paying users yet).
- Offer (assumed): school plan PKR 1,500 per student a month, or a parent plan of PKR 2,500 a month; one-month trial for a school class.
- Assumptions to test: 1) schools will pay per student; 2) kids keep using it without a teacher pushing; 3) parents see value in coding over exam tuition; 4) Urdu support matters.

## AI Clinic Management Assistant
- Idea: an assistant for small clinics that books appointments, sends reminders on WhatsApp in Urdu or English, and keeps simple patient records.
- Target customer: owners of small private clinics and family practices in Pakistan seeing 40 to 120 patients a day.
- Problem and current behavior: receptionists manage a register book and phone calls; patients miss appointments; no records search.
- Evidence: delivered to one clinic for free; it is in use.
- Offer (assumed): monthly plan of PKR 8,000 per clinic including setup and WhatsApp reminders.
- Assumptions to test: 1) the clinic that uses it free will start paying; 2) other clinics trust a small startup with patient data; 3) reminders reduce no-shows enough to matter; 4) receptionists will adopt it.

## Mental Health Companion
- Idea: a supportive chatbot for patients of a mental health clinic between sessions: mood check-ins, coping exercises, reminders, with escalation to the clinic.
- Target customer: psychologists and psychiatric clinics in Pakistan, and their patients.
- Problem and current behavior: patients get little support between sessions; stigma stops many from asking for help; therapists can't follow up with everyone.
- Evidence: built for one clinic, shipping soon.
- Offer (assumed): clinic license of PKR 10,000 a month, free for the clinic's patients.
- Assumptions to test: 1) clinicians trust a chatbot with their patients; 2) patients use it weekly; 3) safety and escalation are good enough for a sensitive use; 4) clinics will pay for it.
